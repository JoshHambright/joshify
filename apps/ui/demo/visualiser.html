Joshify — visualiser

Twenty-six shader passes and seven scenes across fifteen looks, running the real pipeline the Pi will run. Pick a look, then drag Intensity down to zero — every pass is required to be an exact pass-through there, which is what makes the effect dial mean anything.

  • Orbit — a Julia orbit trap where the cover is the trap texture. Turn the cover off and there is no picture, only a two-tone wash. It walks the parameter rather than zooming: fp32 runs out of mantissa about twenty seconds into a zoom, and staying inside the main cardioid guarantees the set can never fall to dust.
  • Reef — the calm mode. The album is the light: the only source is the surface overhead, and what it transmits is the cover. Its fragment shader declares neither uBeat nor uTime, so the stage that decides every colour is structurally incapable of flashing.
  • Solids — the cover twice over, which is what a mid-90s desktop theme actually was: the field behind the solids is the sleeve, and every facet is a crop of that same sleeve.
  • Relief — the cover as a heightfield, flown over. Mirrored rather than repeated, because a repeat puts a cliff at every tile edge and a cliff in a heightfield is a wall.
  • Drift — the cover sampled into depth. One star per 8×8 block of the sleeve, coloured by that block’s hue. The beat streaks rather than flashes, dividing colour by the square root of the stretch so the light on the panel is unchanged.

The pulse is Tier 0: procedural, and not claiming to be the track’s beat — Spotify’s tempo and beat-grid endpoints were withdrawn for new apps. Tap four times to set a tempo and the readout switches to Tier 1. Grain is the render scale, so dropping it is both a 4× saving and the look.
