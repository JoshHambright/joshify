Nightcrawler

A diversion upon the formation of vegetable mould

Snake, but the animal is an earthworm, and the board a vertical section of garden soil twenty inches in depth. It feeds upon fallen leaves, which gather near the surface; it loosens every inch of earth it passes through; and it has no eyes, so that it must remember the burrow behind it. The mould continues at either hand. Above lies the open air, and below, the subsoil ends.

* After Darwin, The Formation of Vegetable Mould through the Action of Worms (London: John Murray, 1881), wherein the worms of an English field are reckoned to pass between ten and eighteen tons of earth to the acre through their bodies in the course of a single year. The castings dropped behind the animal here are that same labour, in little; and the pale swollen band a fifth of the way down its body is the clitellum, by which the cocoon is secreted, and the only sure landmark on a worm.
